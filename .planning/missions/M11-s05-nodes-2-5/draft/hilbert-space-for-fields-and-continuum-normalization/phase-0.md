## Wonder Hook

Two lines from the same page of the same assessment sheet, minutes apart.

The first:

$$\hat{A}\lvert\psi\rangle = \sum_{n}\hat{A}\lvert\varphi_{n}\rangle\langle\varphi_{n}\lvert\psi\rangle = \sum_{n}a_{n}\hat{A}\lvert\varphi_{n}\rangle$$

Clean, correct, produced without hesitation, recorded at grading as **a fluent genuine production** — one of only three across the whole assessment record, alongside the Levi-Civita formula and the geometric-trinity triangle. Whatever four years away from this material cost, it did not cost you Dirac notation.

The second, on being asked in what sense $\lvert x\rangle$ fails to be a vector in the Hilbert space:

> *because it is four-dimensional.*

These are not two levels of knowledge. **They are the same skill one step apart**, and the step is the whole content of this node.

The step, plainly. In the first line the label $n$ runs over a countable set, $\langle\varphi_{n}\lvert\varphi_{m}\rangle = \delta_{nm}$ is a Kronecker delta — a *number* — and each $\lvert\varphi_{n}\rangle$ is an honest unit vector: everything finite, everything a state you could prepare. Make the label continuous and every one of those clauses fails at once. The sum becomes an integral and needs a measure. The Kronecker delta becomes a Dirac delta, which is neither a number nor a function. And the "basis vector" acquires infinite norm: $\langle x\lvert x'\rangle = \delta^{3}(\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}')$ at $\mathbf{x} = \mathbf{x}'$ is $\delta^{3}(0)$, so $\langle x\lvert x\rangle = \infty$.

**That is why $\lvert x\rangle$ is not in $\mathcal{H}$, and it is the whole reason.** A Hilbert space is one in which every vector has finite length; this object has infinite length; therefore it is not one of the vectors. The dimensionality of the label never enters. Swap $\lvert x\rangle$ for $\lvert\mathbf{k}\rangle$, a three-component label, and nothing improves: node 2's algebra gives $\langle\mathbf{k}\lvert\mathbf{k}'\rangle = \langle0\rvert a_{\mathbf{k}}a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}'}\lvert0\rangle = (2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\mathbf{k}-\mathbf{k}')$ — probe item 3 — again infinite at $\mathbf{k}' = \mathbf{k}$. **Every state written down in this module so far, from node 1's $a^{\dagger}_{\mathbf{k}}\lvert0\rangle$ onwards, has had infinite norm.**

**And now the part that makes this a node rather than a correction.** The natural response — "then the formalism is broken" — is wrong. The infinity is not a pathology of field theory: a **plane wave is not normalizable** in ordinary single-particle quantum mechanics for exactly this reason, and nobody regards $\psi(x) = e^{ikx}$ as a crisis. What everyone does in practice is treat plane waves as computational kernels — expand in them, integrate against them, and let the physical state be the packet that comes out. The structure making this legitimate rather than a habit is a **rigged Hilbert space**: a triple $\Phi\subset\mathcal{H}\subset\Phi'$ in which the improper states live in $\Phi'$ as distributions and act on the well-behaved states of $\Phi$ by integration. Dirac's notation was built around that structure before the mathematics existed to justify it, which is why it keeps working when the naive reading of it does not.

Two things fall out, both used later:

**"Spectrum" and "eigenbasis" come apart**, and the assessment caught them together. The spectrum of $\hat{x}$ is $\mathbb{R}$ — every real number, continuously — and $\hat{x}$ has **no eigenvectors in $\mathcal{H}$ whatsoever**. Those are consistent, and the definition that makes them consistent (a spectral value is one you can approach arbitrarily closely with normalized states, not one you can hit) is what the Structural Stage is for. It is also what makes hydrogen's mixed spectrum — bound states you can hit, scattering states you cannot — one object rather than two.

**And the fence, which is deliberate.** There is a genuinely hard question next door: on *which* states is an unbounded operator defined, when is a symmetric operator self-adjoint, and what is the family of choices when it is not? Your Block E oral put that at true zero and Gate 6 scheduled it as module **B2**, cold and just-in-time before S2.1. **This node does not teach it and does not need it.** Norms and completeness are prior to domains: you can know exactly why $\lvert\mathbf{k}\rangle$ is improper, and how to work with it, without knowing what a deficiency index is.

By the end you will write the continuum resolution of the identity from memory with its measure, compute the norm of a packet and of the improper state it is built from, say precisely what $\delta^{3}(0)$ is, and state — in one sentence, about the state and not about the label — why a plane wave is something you compute with and never something you prepare.
