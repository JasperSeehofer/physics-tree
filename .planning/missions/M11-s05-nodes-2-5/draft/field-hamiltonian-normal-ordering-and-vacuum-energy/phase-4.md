## Self Explanation Prompt

Prose, your own words, without equations wherever you can manage, without looking back at Phase 2. Three or four paragraphs. The test of a good answer: would it persuade a physicist who can normal-order fluently and believes that doing so disposes of the vacuum energy?

**Explain what the zero-point divergence is, why deleting it is legitimate, and what exactly the legitimacy depends on.**

Address all four of the following, and make the causal order explicit.

1. **Where the term comes from and what kind of object it is.** Name the step that produced it, and be precise about which relation was used and at what value of its arguments. Then say what kind of mathematical object the result is — not "infinite", which is a property, but what *sort* of thing — and why that classification is what makes the rest of the argument possible.

2. **The two infinities.** The c-number is a product of two divergent factors. Say what each one is an infinity *of*, which of the two disappears in a finite box, and why treating them as one object makes the whole phenomenon sound like an artefact when only half of it is.

3. **The licence, and its fourth clause.** In Phase 1 Part C1 you wrote a justification for the deletion before seeing this node's. Get it out and read it. Then write the corrected version as a chain of clauses, and identify which single clause is a claim about physics rather than about algebra. Say what would have to be true of the world for that clause to fail — and then note that it *is* false, and name the interaction that makes it so.

4. **Why the momentum operator is the control experiment.** The identical reordering on $\mathbf{P}$ produces nothing at all. Explain why, in one sentence, and then say what that fact rules out: name at least two explanations of the vacuum energy that the $\mathbf{P}$ calculation refutes.

Finish with one sentence answering: **is the vacuum energy of a free scalar field a prediction of the theory, a convention, or an artefact?** Commit to one, and say what observation would change your answer.

## Reflection Questions

1. **The Casimir question, sharpened.** The Casimir force is measured, is derived from zero-point energy, and is finite.

   (i) Say precisely what quantity the experiment measures, and why it is finite when the thing it is computed from is not. (ii) Phase 3's Full Example found that the divergent piece was proportional to the length of the region. Explain why that proportionality is exactly what makes the subtraction unambiguous, and construct a hypothetical in which it would *not* be — a comparison between two configurations whose divergent pieces differed. (iii) There is a literature deriving the Casimir force with no reference to zero-point energy at all, from retarded van der Waals forces between the charges in the plates. Given that both routes give the same number, what does the experiment actually establish about the reality of vacuum energy? Be careful: the answer is neither "everything" nor "nothing".

2. **What normal ordering does not do.** You proved $\varphi(x)\varphi(y) = \,:\!\varphi(x)\varphi(y)\!: + \langle0\rvert\varphi(x)\varphi(y)\lvert0\rangle$.

   (i) Use it to explain why "normal-ordered products are symmetric, so ordering no longer matters" is false, and be specific about which object the ordering-dependence moved *into*. (ii) The correction term is a c-number for a *free* field. Which property of the free field made it so, and would you expect the same statement in an interacting theory? (iii) Node 2's $\Delta(x-y)$ and the correction term here are built from the same integrand. State the relation between them, and say what has to be added to the correction term to turn it into the object node 9 calls the propagator.

3. **Where the licence expires, and why it is your subject.** Three failures were named: interactions, no preferred vacuum, and gravity. They are not equally serious.

   Rank them, and justify the ranking by saying, for each, whether what fails is *the operation*, *the argument*, or *the physics*. Then take the third one seriously in one paragraph. A quantity that every laboratory experiment is blind to is measured directly by cosmology, and the two answers disagree by at least $10^{56}$ at scales that have been tested. **Is that a failure of quantum field theory, a failure of general relativity, or a failure of the assumption that the two can be used together in this way?** Argue for one; note which of the three would be least comfortable for the programme this module belongs to; and say what you would want to see measured before committing.
